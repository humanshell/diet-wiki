Calisthenics is a form of resistance training that uses an individual's body weight as the primary source of resistance. Derived from the Greek *kállos* (beauty) and *sthenos* (strength), it encompasses multi-joint, compound movements such as push-ups, squats, lunges, pull-ups, planks, and burpees — typically requiring little to no equipment.

As a core component of physical activity for [secondary-cardiovascular-prevention](../concepts/secondary-cardiovascular-prevention.md), calisthenics provides a low-barrier, progressively adaptable exercise modality that simultaneously builds muscular strength, cardiovascular endurance, and functional mobility.

## Cardiovascular Benefits

### Blood Pressure Reduction

Resistance training, including calisthenics, independently reduces both systolic and diastolic blood pressure. The AHA 2023 Scientific Statement on Resistance Exercise Training (Paluch et al.) confirmed that regular resistance training lowers resting blood pressure through improved endothelial function, reduced systemic vascular resistance, and enhanced nitric oxide bioavailability. Post-exercise hypotension — a sustained blood pressure reduction lasting up to 2 hours after a session — is particularly well-documented for bodyweight resistance exercise.

### Improved Lipid Profile

Consistent calisthenics training has been associated with favorable changes in blood lipids: reduced triglycerides, increased HDL-C, and lower LDL-C. These changes compound the [dyslipidemia-management](../concepts/dyslipidemia-management.md) achieved through the [mediterranean-diet](mediterranean-diet.md).

### Enhanced Glycemic Control

Bodyweight resistance training improves insulin sensitivity and glucose disposal. The large muscle mass engaged in compound calisthenic movements (squats, lunges, push-ups) promotes glucose uptake into skeletal muscle independent of insulin, reducing postprandial hyperglycemia and the metabolic stress that drives atherosclerotic progression.

### VO₂max Improvement

Structured calisthenics training — particularly when performed in circuit format with minimal rest — produces meaningful increases in maximal oxygen uptake (VO₂max). A 2024 study (Imran et al., *Indonesian Journal of Public Health and Nutrition*) found that calisthenics training significantly improved estimated VO₂max values in sedentary adults. Higher VO₂max is independently associated with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality.

### Heart Rate Variability and Autonomic Function

Regular resistance exercise improves cardiac autonomic modulation, shifting the balance toward parasympathetic (vagal) dominance. Improved heart rate variability is associated with reduced arrhythmic risk and better cardiovascular recovery after exertion — both relevant for post-MI patients.

### Inflammatory and Oxidative Stress Reduction

Calisthenics training reduces systemic inflammatory markers including C-reactive protein (CRP) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), while upregulating antioxidant enzymes. This complements the [anti-inflammatory-environment](../concepts/anti-inflammatory-environment.md) promoted by a Mediterranean dietary pattern.

## Role in Cardiac Rehabilitation

Calisthenics is ideally suited for supervised cardiac rehabilitation and home-based exercise maintenance:

- **Early post-MI phase (2–4 weeks)**: Low-intensity bodyweight movements (wall push-ups, seated leg raises, partial squats) can begin within the first month after an event, per AHA/ACC guidelines for resistance training in cardiac patients
- **Progressive overload**: Difficulty is increased by adding repetitions, reducing rest, changing leverage (e.g., incline → flat → decline push-ups), or moving to single-limb variations — no equipment upgrade needed
- **Combined aerobic + resistance**: Circuit-style calisthenics (30–60 seconds work, 15–30 seconds rest) develops cardiovascular endurance and muscular strength simultaneously, mirroring the combined training approach recommended by the 2024 AHA Scientific Statement on Cardiac Rehabilitation Components
- **No equipment barrier**: Exercises can be performed anywhere, eliminating access as a barrier to adherence — a critical factor in long-term [lifestyle-modification](../concepts/lifestyle-modification.md)

## Key Principles for Post-MI Patients

| Principle | Application |
|---|---|
| **Start low, go slow** | Begin with 1–2 sets of 8–10 reps of basic exercises (wall push-ups, chair squats) |
| **Avoid breath-holding** | Exhale on exertion; the Valsalva maneuver raises intrathoracic pressure and cardiac afterload |
| **Monitor perceived exertion** | Use RPE 11–14 (Borg scale, "fairly light" to "somewhat hard") |
| **Circuit format preferred** | 30–60 sec work / 15–30 sec rest keeps heart rate elevated for aerobic benefit |
| **Frequency** | 2–3 non-consecutive days per week (per AHA resistance training guidelines) |
| **Precede with warm-up** | 5–10 minutes of walking, arm circles, and dynamic stretching |
| **Stop signal** | Cease exercise for chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, dizziness, or palpitations |

## Common Exercises and Progression

| Exercise | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push-up | Wall push-up | Incline (counter) push-up | Full floor push-up / Decline push-up |
| Squat | Chair squat (partial) | Full bodyweight squat | Pistol squat (single-leg) |
| Lunge | Static lunge (hold support) | Walking lunge | Jump lunge |
| Plank | Knee plank | Full plank (30–60 sec) | Plank with leg lift |
| Pull-up | Negative (eccentric) pull-up | Band-assisted pull-up | Full pull-up / Archer pull-up |
| Glute bridge | Floor bridge (both legs) | Single-leg bridge | Weighted bridge |
| Burpee | Step-back burpee (no jump) | Full burpee | Burpee with push-up |

## Synergy with Mediterranean Diet

The combination of calisthenics resistance training and a Mediterranean dietary pattern produces multiplicative cardiovascular benefits:

- Calisthenics improves insulin sensitivity; the Med diet provides stable glycemic fuel
- Resistance training builds lean mass, raising resting metabolic rate; the Med diet supplies the protein, healthy fats, and micronutrients needed for tissue repair
- Exercise reduces systemic inflammation; Med diet polyphenols and omega-3s target the same pathways through complementary mechanisms
- Post-exercise muscle repair requires adequate protein; Med pattern sources include [legumes](legumes.md), [fish](fish.md), and moderate [cheese](cheese.md) and [yogurt](yogurt.md)

## Research Highlights

- **AHA 2023 Scientific Statement** (Paluch et al., *Circulation*): Resistance exercise training is recommended as part of a comprehensive CV prevention program; benefits include BP reduction, improved lipid profile, and enhanced insulin sensitivity
- **Cardiac rehab guidelines** (AHA 2024, *Circulation*): Progressive resistance exercise independently improves strength, balance, metabolism, and functional independence in cardiac patients; can begin within 2–4 weeks of starting aerobic exercise
- **Calisthenics and VO₂max** (Imran et al., 2024): Calisthenics training significantly improved estimated VO₂max in previously sedentary adults
- **Body composition** (2017 study): Calisthenics training improves posture, strength, and body composition without any equipment
- **Older adults** (2015 study): Calisthenics significantly improved flexibility and neuromuscular function in elderly participants over one year of training

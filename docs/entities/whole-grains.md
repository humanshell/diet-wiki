Whole grains are cereal grains consumed in their complete form — bran, germ, and endosperm intact — making them a foundational component of the [[mediterranean-diet]]. Common varieties include wheat, barley, oats, bulgur, farro, spelt, and whole-grain pasta and bread.

## Cardiovascular Benefits

Whole grains are strongly associated with cardiovascular protection through multiple mechanisms:

- **Fiber and beta-glucan**: Soluble fiber, particularly beta-glucan in oats and barley, reduces LDL-C by binding bile acids and increasing fecal excretion
- **Blood pressure reduction**: Regular whole grain consumption is associated with lower systolic and diastolic blood pressure
- **Inflammation reduction**: Whole grains reduce systemic inflammation markers including CRP
- **Glycemic control**: Low glycemic index whole grains reduce postprandial glucose spikes, supporting weight management and insulin sensitivity
- **Gut microbiome**: Fermentation of whole grain fiber produces short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) that support arterial health

## Mediterranean Context

The traditional Mediterranean dietary pattern emphasizes unprocessed cereals — primarily as bread, bulgur, and whole-grain porridges — as a daily staple. These are consumed with olive oil, legumes, and vegetables rather than with butter, cream, or processed meats.

## Research Highlights
- A 2016 meta-analysis of 45 studies found that 3 servings of whole grains daily reduced cardiovascular disease risk by 20–30%
- The [[lyon-diet-heart-study]] emphasized bread as a primary component of the Mediterranean intervention group, consumed daily alongside vegetables, fish, and fruit
- Whole grains are a key component of the [[anti-inflammatory-environment]] promoted by the Mediterranean dietary pattern

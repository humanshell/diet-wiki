Yogurt is a fermented dairy product made by culturing milk with beneficial bacteria (primarily Lactobacillus and Streptococcus species). It is a traditional component of the Mediterranean diet, particularly in Greece and the Levant, where it is consumed as a breakfast food, a sauce base (tzatziki), or a snack with nuts and fruit.

## Nutritional Profile

Yogurt provides high-quality protein, calcium, potassium, and B vitamins. Greek yogurt — strained to remove whey — has approximately double the protein content of conventional yogurt per serving. Plain, unsweetened yogurt is preferred in the Mediterranean context; sweetened or flavored varieties introduce added sugars that are otherwise minimized.

## Cardiovascular and Metabolic Benefits

The fermented nature of yogurt provides benefits beyond its basic nutritional content:

- **Probiotic effects**: Yogurt contains live cultures that support gut microbiome diversity. A healthy gut microbiome is associated with reduced [[systemic-inflammation]] and improved lipid profiles
- **Blood pressure reduction**: The combination of potassium, calcium, and probiotic peptides in yogurt has been associated with modest reductions in systolic blood pressure
- **Glycemic control**: Yogurt consumption is associated with improved insulin sensitivity and lower postprandial glucose, supporting weight management
- **Calcium and [[blood-pressure-reduction]]**: Like cheese, yogurt provides calcium and potassium in a format that supports electrolyte balance and vascular health

## Mediterranean Context

In the traditional Mediterranean diet, full-fat plain yogurt was a common breakfast item, often served with honey, nuts, or figs. Modern Mediterranean dietary recommendations support regular consumption of plain yogurt as part of the dairy component, emphasizing whole-food preparation over sweetened varieties.

# **2026 ACC/AHA Multisociety Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia**

**Source URL:** [American College of Cardiology / American Heart Association 2026 Guideline](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/journal-scans/2026/03/13/15/20/acc-aha-release-new-clinical-guideline-for-managing-dyslipidemia)

## **Executive Summary**

The 2026 multisociety guideline consolidates evidence-based recommendations for evaluating, managing, and monitoring lipids to reduce atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk. It marks a significant transition by restoring strict LDL-C treatment goals based on risk stratification and emphasizing earlier lifestyle and pharmacological interventions.

## **Key Clinical Directives**

### **1\. Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDL-C) Targets**

* **Very High-Risk ASCVD (Secondary Prevention):** Target LDL-C is set strictly to \<55 mg/dL. This applies directly to patients recovering from a recent acute myocardial infarction.  
* **Clinical ASCVD (Not at Very High-Risk):** Target LDL-C is \<70 mg/dL.  
* **Primary Prevention:** Target LDL-C is \<100 mg/dL for borderline/intermediate risk and \<70 mg/dL for high-risk patients.

### **2\. Preferred Diagnostic & Measurement Formulas**

* Standard lipid panels should utilize either the **Martin/Hopkins equation** or the **Sampson/NIH equation** rather than the legacy Friedewald formula to estimate LDL-C, particularly when levels are aggressively lowered to \<70 mg/dL.

### **3\. Advanced Biomarkers for Residual Risk Assessment**

* **Lipoprotein(a) \[Lp(a)\]:** Universal screening is recommended at least once in adulthood. Levels \>=125 nmol/L (or \>=50 mg/dL) represent a 1.4-fold increased risk of MACE, and levels \>=250 nmol/L indicate a twofold or greater risk. Since Lp(a) is genetically determined, lifestyle changes do not significantly alter its concentration; identifying high levels guides aggressive modification of all other risk factors.  
* **Apolipoprotein B (ApoB):** Recommended for adults on lipid-lowering therapy (LLT), particularly those with established ASCVD, type 2 diabetes, cardio-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome, or elevated triglycerides (TG). ApoB represents the total count of atherogenic particles and helps guide therapy when traditional LDL-C targets appear met.  
* **High-Sensitivity C-Reactive Protein (hsCRP):** If measured at \>=2 mg/L on two successive occasions in borderline-risk patients, it identifies residual inflammatory risk and supports initiating high-intensity statin therapy to stabilize coronary lesions.

### **4\. Dietary Supplements and Referrals**

* **Unregulated Supplements:** The guideline explicitly recommends *against* using dietary supplements (e.g., fish oils, red yeast rice) to manage dyslipidemia or lower LDL-C/TG, citing inconsistent data and a lack of proven reduction in ASCVD risk.  
* **Registered Dietitian Nutritionist (RDN) Referral:** Class 1 recommendation for fasting triglycerides \>=1000 mg/dL to prevent pancreatitis. Class 2a recommendation for fasting triglycerides between 150 and 999 mg/dL in patients with CKM syndrome or diabetes to implement evidence-based cardioprotective diets.

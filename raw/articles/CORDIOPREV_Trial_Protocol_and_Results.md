# **The CORDIOPREV Trial: Mediterranean Diet vs. Low-Fat Diet in Secondary Prevention**

**Source URL:**([https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(22)00122-2/fulltext](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736\(22\)00122-2/fulltext))

## **Study Overview**

The CORDIOPREV trial was a landmark, single-center, randomized, controlled trial conducted at the Reina Sofia University Hospital in Córdoba, Spain. It randomized 1,002 patients with established coronary heart disease (CHD) into two intensive, high-adherence dietary arms for a 7-year follow-up period to evaluate their efficacy in preventing recurrent major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE).

## **Dietary Arms & Parameters**

### **1\. Mediterranean Diet Group (502 Patients)**

* **Macronutrients:** Fat comprised \>=35% of total calories (specifically 22% monounsaturated fatty acids \[MUFAs\] and 6% polyunsaturated fatty acids \[PUFAs\]), protein comprised 15%, and carbohydrates were restricted to \<50% of total daily energy.  
* **Primary Foods:** 40 to 60 grams of extra-virgin olive oil (EVOO) per day; \>=3 servings of fatty fish per week; \>=3 servings of tree nuts per week.  
* **Constraints:** No energy/caloric restriction was enforced, and no physical activity was prescribed.

### **2\. Low-Fat Diet Group (500 Patients)**

* **Macronutrients:** Fat was restricted to \<30% of total calories (12-14% MUFAs, 6-8% PUFAs), protein comprised 15%, and complex carbohydrates comprised \>=55% of total energy.  
* **Primary Foods:** Low-fat dairy 2 to 3 times per day; lean fish was preferred over fatty fish; nuts were limited; EVOO was restricted to 20 to 30 grams of sunflower or regular olive oil per day.  
* **Constraints:** Identical to the Mediterranean group (no caloric restriction or physical activity prescribed).

## **Trial Results and Clinical Endpoints**

### **1\. Primary MACE Outcome**

* The primary composite endpoint included myocardial infarction, revascularization, ischemic stroke, peripheral artery disease (PAD), and cardiovascular death.  
* Over 7 years, MACE occurred in **17.3%** of the Mediterranean group compared to **22.2%** of the low-fat group.  
* In the fully adjusted model, the Mediterranean diet demonstrated a **28.1% lower risk** of recurrent MACE compared to the low-fat diet ($P \= 0.024$).  
* **Sex-Based Differences:** The risk reduction was highly pronounced in men (16.2% vs 22.8% event rate). No statistically significant difference was found in women, which the authors attributed to a lack of statistical power (women made up only 17.5% of the cohort).

### **2\. Kidney Function Preservation (Secondary Analysis)**

* Over the 7-year follow-up, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) declined in both groups compared to baseline.  
* However, the Mediterranean diet significantly attenuated the decline of eGFR compared to the low-fat diet across the entire cohort ($P \= 0.033$).  
* This kidney-protective effect was highly pronounced in patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus ($P \= 0.040$) and in those with mildly-impaired eGFR at baseline ($P \= 0.002$), proving that a high-MUFA Mediterranean diet slows chronic kidney disease progression in coronary patients.

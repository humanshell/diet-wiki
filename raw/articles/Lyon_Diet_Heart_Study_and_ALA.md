# **The Lyon Diet Heart Study: Secondary Prevention and the Role of Alpha-Linolenic Acid (ALA)**

**Source URL:**([https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.103.13.1823](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.103.13.1823))

## **Study Overview**

The Lyon Diet Heart Study was a randomized, single-blind secondary prevention trial designed to test whether a Mediterranean-style diet could reduce the recurrence of cardiac events compared to a standard prudent Western-type diet after a first myocardial infarction. Patients were followed for an average of 46 months.

## **Dietary Modifications**

The experimental group followed a unique Mediterranean pattern with several strict instructions:

* **Margarine Modification:** Butter and cream were entirely replaced with a specialized canola-oil-based margarine supplied by the study. This margarine was comparable to olive oil in saturated fat (15% of energy) and oleic acid (48% of energy), but was uniquely rich in **alpha-linolenic acid (ALA)**, an essential omega-3 fatty acid, containing 4.8% ALA compared to just 0.6% in typical olive oil.  
* **Food Choices:** Emphasized a significantly higher intake of bread, root and green vegetables, fish, and fruit (at least once daily). Red meat (beef, pork, lamb) was strictly limited and replaced with poultry.  
* **Lipid and Risk Factors:** Interestingly, plasma lipids (including total cholesterol, LDL-C, and HDL-C), blood pressure, body mass index, and smoking status remained identical between the experimental and control groups throughout the trial.

## **Primary and Secondary Outcomes**

* **Cardioprotective Benefit:** Despite having identical circulating cholesterol levels, the Mediterranean-ALA group experienced an unprecedented **50% to 70% reduction in recurrent myocardial infarction and cardiac death**.  
* **Composite Endpoints:** Composite Endpoint 1 (cardiac death and nonfatal MI) was reduced (14 events vs 44 in the control group). Adjusted risk ratios for major adverse events ranged from 0.28 to 0.53.  
* **Mechanism:** The study proved that a Mediterranean diet protects the myocardium through non-lipid pathways, primarily by establishing an anti-thrombotic and anti-inflammatory environment promoted by the high intake of oleic acid, ALA, and antioxidants, and a lower intake of saturated and omega-6 fatty acids.  
* **Synergistic Action with Omega-3:** The clinical literature notes that combining a Mediterranean diet rich in ALA (aiming for 2g/day or 0.6% to 1% of daily energy) with 1g/day of long-chain omega-3 fatty acid supplements decreases cardiac death by 30% and sudden cardiac death by 45%.

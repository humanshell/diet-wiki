# **The Ornish Lifestyle Heart Trial: Quantitative Angiography and Plaque Regression**

**Source URL:**([https://www.uclahealth.org/sites/default/files/documents/Ornish\_LiteratureReview.pdf?f=90b7156a](https://www.uclahealth.org/sites/default/files/documents/Ornish_LiteratureReview.pdf?f=90b7156a))

## **Study Overview**

The Ornish Lifestyle Heart Trial was the first randomized, controlled clinical trial to evaluate whether intensive, non-pharmacological lifestyle modifications could motivate patients to achieve and sustain direct, angiographically-documented regression of severe coronary atherosclerosis. Forty-eight patients with one-, two-, or three-vessel coronary artery disease were randomized to an experimental lifestyle group or a usual-care control group and followed for 5 years.

## **Interventions in the Experimental Group**

The trial mandated a comprehensive program consisting of four co-equal elements:

1. **Very Low-Fat Vegetarian Diet:** A whole-foods vegetarian diet containing approximately **10% of total calories from fat**. No animal products were permitted except egg whites and up to 1 cup per day of non-fat milk or yogurt. Oils, butter, margarine, avocados, nuts, and seeds were entirely excluded to meet the 10% fat target.  
2. **Aerobic Exercise:** Moderate aerobic exercise for at least 3 hours per week.  
3. **Stress Management:** Stress management training (meditation, yoga, progressive relaxation) for 1 hour per day.  
4. **Group Support:** Psychosocial support groups twice weekly.

## **Quantitative Angiography Results**

Arterial plaque was measured using computer-assisted quantitative coronary angiography at baseline, 1 year, and 5 years.

* **1-Year Follow-up:** Average percent diameter stenosis regressed from 40.0% to 37.8% in the experimental group, while progressing from 42.7% to 46.1% in the usual-care control group.  
* **5-Year Follow-up:** The experimental group showed further plaque regression, with average stenosis decreasing to **37.3%** (a 7.9% relative improvement from baseline). Conversely, the control group experienced significant, continuous progression of stenosis to **51.9%** (a 27.7% relative worsening) ($P \= 0.001$ between groups).  
* **Adherence Correlation:** There was a strong, direct correlation between the degree of program adherence and the amount of plaque regression achieved at both 1 and 5 years.  
* **Clinical Events:** Cardiac events occurred significantly more frequently in the control group (risk ratio of **2.47** for any major clinical cardiac event in the control group compared to the experimental group). Angina frequency decreased by 72% in the experimental group.

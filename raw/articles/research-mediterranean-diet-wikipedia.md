# Mediterranean Diet Research Summary

Proposed in 1975 by Ancel Keys and Margaret Keys, inspired by eating habits of Greece, Italy, Southern France, Spain, and the Levant in the late 1950s-early 1960s.

Core characteristics:
- Emphasizes plant-based foods: unprocessed cereals, legumes, vegetables, fruits
- Moderate consumption of fish and dairy (mostly cheese and yogurt)
- Low amounts of red meat, refined grains, and sugar
- Olive oil is principal source of fat
- Wine consumed in low to moderate amounts, usually with meals
- Low in saturated fat, high in monounsaturated fat and dietary fiber

Health outcomes:
- Associated with reduction in all-cause mortality in observational studies
- 2017 review: lowers risk of heart disease and early death
- May help with weight loss in obese people
- One of three healthy diets recommended in 2015-2020 U.S. Dietary Guidelines
- Recognized by WHO as healthy eating pattern
- UNESCO recognized Mediterranean diet as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2010
- 2014 meta-analysis: elevated olive oil consumption associated with reduced risk of all-cause mortality, cardiovascular events and stroke
- EFSA approved health claims on olive oil polyphenols for protection against oxidation of blood lipids
- 2020 review: may optimize cardiovascular health
- 2023 review: reduction of mortality and CVD risk in women
- 2024 meta-analysis: adherence associated with reduction in cardiovascular disease risk
- 2013 meta-analysis: strict adherence correlated with decreased risk of dying from cancer by 6%
- 2021 review: associated with 13% lower risk of cancer mortality
- 2016 review: relation between greater adherence and better cognitive performance
- 2013 review: greater adherence correlated with lower risk of Alzheimer's and slower cognitive decline
